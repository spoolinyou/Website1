When we started The Missing Link, we expected the hard part to be the volume — more than a thousand open missing person cases in Indiana alone. The volume is real. But the harder problem is the one nobody warns you about: **the records don't agree with each other.**

## What the data actually looks like

Take one case and look it up in three places. In NamUs, the date of last contact might be listed one way. The Charley Project profile, lovingly maintained by volunteers, may carry a different spelling of a middle name. A county record obtained through an APRA request may show a case number that appears nowhere else. None of these sources is wrong, exactly — they were built at different times, by different hands, for different purposes. But a researcher, a detective, or a grieving parent searching for a name shouldn't have to reconcile them alone.

## Our method

For every Indiana case, we are building a single verified entry with:

- Name variants and confirmed spellings
- Date and location of last contact, sourced
- Every database where the case appears — and where it's missing
- The investigating agency and Indiana State Police district
- The public records trail we used to verify it

Mapping cases by ISP district is more than tidy organization. It shows coverage: which districts have cases missing from national databases, and where a family may need help getting a NamUs profile completed. If that's your family, our [missing persons program](/programs/missing-persons) will help, free.

## Where we are

The compilation phase is well underway, and Indiana is only the start — Ohio, Illinois, Michigan, and Kentucky are next, because missing people don't respect state lines and the data shouldn't either.

If you can give a few careful hours a week, the [Missing Link data team](/get-involved/volunteer) is where volunteers make the most immediate difference. And if you can [fund a records request](/donate), you are quite literally paying for answers.
