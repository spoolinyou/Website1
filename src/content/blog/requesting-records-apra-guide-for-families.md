Indiana's Access to Public Records Act — APRA — is the law that lets you ask government agencies for their records. For families of missing persons and unsolved deaths, it is one of the most powerful tools you have, and one of the least explained. Here is what we wish every family knew, in plain language.

## What you can ask for

Under APRA (Indiana Code 5-14-3), you can request records from state and local agencies: incident and case reports, dispatch logs, 911 records, coroner reports, autopsy conclusions, and correspondence. You do not have to explain why you want a record, and you do not have to be a lawyer to ask.

## What agencies can withhold

Be prepared: **investigatory records of law enforcement agencies can be withheld** while a case is considered active — this is the exception families run into most. But "can be withheld" is not "must be withheld," and plenty of records fall outside the exception: the initial incident report, dispatch logs, and coroner's verdicts are frequently releasable. A denial of one record is not a denial of all of them.

## How to ask well

1. Put it in writing (email counts) and date it.
2. Be specific: name, date range, incident location, case number if you have one.
3. Ask for records, not answers — "all dispatch logs for [address] on [date]," not "why didn't anyone respond sooner?"
4. Keep every response. Denials must cite the statutory exception they rely on.
5. If you're denied, you can file a complaint with Indiana's Public Access Counselor — it's free, and their opinions carry weight.
