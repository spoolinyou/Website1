There is a version of this story I tell at speaking events, with dates and case details in order. This is not that version. This is the one about why an organization exists.

My mother, Leslie "Hope" Houston, was killed in October 2024. What I remember about the months after is not one big injustice — it's a hundred small closed doors. Records I didn't know I could ask for. Phone calls that weren't returned. The particular silence that settles over a case when everyone official has moved on and the family has not.

So I did the work myself. For seven months I requested records, built timelines, organized every scrap of evidence into something no one could ignore. I was not a detective. I was a daughter with a spreadsheet and a refusal. And it worked — the case was reopened, and charges were filed.

I would trade all of it to have her back. Since I can't, the question became: what do I do with what I learned?

The answer is Silver Lining of Hope. Everything we do — the [cold case reviews](/programs/homicides), the [missing persons research](/programs/missing-persons), [The Missing Link](/programs/the-missing-link), the [advocacy beside families](/programs/outreach-advocacy) — is the help I needed and couldn't find, offered free to the next family, and the one after that.

Her middle name was Hope. We put it in our pillars, in our name, in everything. Not as a sentiment — as a commitment.

If you're in the place I was in, start [here](/get-help/families). You are not imagining what you've noticed, and you are not alone in it anymore.
